# Therapist Field Guide: Preparation Meetings (Preparation Phase)

> **OSPAT / MAPS Protocol Standard:**
> The preparation phase includes a minimum of 1–2 in-person or online meetings, 60–90 minutes each, before the session day.
> The core principle of preparation: **Safety is born from predictability.** The deeper the therapeutic rapport is built and neurotic expectations are removed, the cleaner and more profound the immersion will be.

---

## 1. Preparation Session Map (Check-in & Rapport)

### Step 1. Building a "Safety Container" (Holding Environment)
- The facilitator does not occupy the position of an all-knowing expert. The position is: a stable ally, witness, and grounding anchor.
- Directly address confidentiality: everything that happens in this space stays between you.
- Complete and discuss the screening questionnaire: re-verify cardiovascular risk factors, medications, and family psychiatric history.

### Step 2. Exploring Personal Mythology and Defense Mechanisms
- What habitual strategies do you use under stress? *(Intellectualization, escape into action, numbing, joking, angry outbursts).*
- "If during the session a strong urge to control everything arises — how will we recognize that this is fear speaking?"
- Identify "somatic anchors": what in the body feels like a safe place?

---

## 2. Deconstructing the Request: From Ego Goals to Processual Intention

| Client's Ego-Driven Expectation | Professional Facilitator Reframe (Unpriming) |
|---------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| *"I want to get rid of anxiety/depression once and for all"* | "We are not declaring war on anxiety. We will go toward the source of this anxiety and ask what it is protecting you from." |
| *"I want to experience samadhi / see the cosmos / like in trip reports"* | "Remove all scenarios. The brain will show not what the ego wants, but where the greatest tension has accumulated. Are you prepared to meet ordinary sadness?" |
| *"Please fix me"* | "Healing is produced by your own Inner Healing Intelligence. I am simply holding space and securing the shore." |

---

## 3. Psychoeducation: The Anatomy of Immersion

The therapist must explain clearly what to expect on a somatic level:
1. **Wave-like character:** the experience moves in waves (surge — pause — next surge). No peak state lasts forever.
2. **Autonomic discharge:** chills, body trembling, spontaneous deep sighs, tears, yawning, or mild nausea — these are healthy discharges of frozen traumatic energy in the autonomic nervous system (per Peter Levine). Don't suppress the tremor — allow the body to shake it through.
3. **Time distortion:** a minute can feel like an eternity. Instruction for the client: "If it seems like time has stopped, remember: pharmacokinetics have an exact physiological limit. This is temporary, and it will pass."

---

## 4. Briefing on Working with Difficult Material: "In and Through"

The therapist teaches the client the key facilitation triad:
1. **Trust:** trust your unconscious and the process.
2. **Let Go:** when you try to control, panic escalates; releasing the grip is the only path to relief.
3. **Be Open:** turn toward the frightening image.

> **Therapist's anchoring words for the client:**
> *"If a monster, an abyss, a room of fear, or a figure from your past appears before you — do not run. In an altered state, flight intensifies the nightmare. Step directly into the center of the monster. Move close and ask: 'Who are you? What have you come to teach me?' You will be surprised how quickly the vision dissolves, transforming into pure power."*

---

## 5. Touch Consent Protocol

Touch in a vulnerable state is a powerful therapeutic tool requiring strict ethics:
- **No spontaneous intrusions:** every touch is discussed in advance and verbally confirmed in the moment.
- **Permitted grounding zones:** client's palm (hand in hand), shoulder, feet (through blanket/socks for connection to the floor).
- **"Touch test" practice at the preparation meeting:**
  - Therapist places a hand on the client's shoulder for 10 seconds.
  - Check-in: "How does that feel? Does it create any tightness? Would you like this contact to be available during the session?"
- **Stop signal:** a simple word or hand gesture meaning "Remove your hand / step back."
