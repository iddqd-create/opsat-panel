# Therapist Field Guide: Preparation Meetings (Preparation Phase)

> **OSPAT Protocol Standard:**  
> The preparation phase includes a minimum of 1–2 in-person or online meetings (60–90 minutes each; optimally 2–3 meetings) before session day.  
> **Safety is built on predictability and transparent informed consent.** A clear therapeutic alliance and realistic expectations create the foundation for a safe session.

---

## 1. Preparation Session Map (Check-in & Rapport)

### Step 1. Building a Safety Container & Screening
- The facilitator does not take the stance of an all-knowing guru; the role is a steady ally, witness, and anchor of physical and emotional safety.
- **Limits of confidentiality:** Explicitly state that session content is confidential, **with standard legal and ethical exceptions** (imminent risk of harm to self or others, or mandatory reporting situations).
- **Screening re-verification:**
  - *Psychiatric exclusions:* personal history of psychosis, **Bipolar I and Bipolar II**, **first-degree family history of psychosis or Bipolar I**.
  - *Medications & somatic risks:* **lithium** (absolute contraindication due to high seizure risk), **MAOIs**, **tramadol**, **SSRIs/SNRIs** (pharmacological blunting requiring supervised taper), **triptans**, uncontrolled hypertension (BP >= 140/90 mmHg).

### Step 2. Exploring Defense Mechanisms & Grounding Anchors
- What habitual strategies do you use under stress? *(Intellectualization, hyper-control, numbing, avoidance, humor, anger).*
- "If a strong urge to control everything arises during the session, how will we recognize that fear is driving it?"
- Identify somatic anchors: what in the body feels steady and safe?

---

## 2. From Rigid Expectations to Open Intention (Unpriming)

| Client's Rigid Expectation | Facilitator Reframe |
|---|---|
| *"I want to get rid of anxiety/depression in one session"* | "We are not declaring war on anxiety. The goal is to explore its roots and understand what it has been trying to protect you from." |
| *"I want to see the cosmos / mystical enlightenment like in trip reports"* | "Set aside all scripts. The mind brings up not what the ego wants, but where tension is held. Are you willing to meet ordinary sadness as well?" |
| *"Please fix me"* | "The core inner work happens within you; my role is to maintain a safe space, medical oversight, and steady support throughout." |

---

## 3. Psychoeducation: Physiology & Session Dynamics

Explain clearly what to expect on a somatic and subjective level:
1. **Wave-like character:** Experience moves in waves (surge — pause — next surge). No peak state remains constant.
2. **Autonomic and somatic responses:** Chills, trembling, deep sighs, tears, yawning, or nausea (driven by peripheral $5\text{-HT}_{3}$ gut receptor activation as well as autonomic release). Do not forcefully suppress trembling or tears.
3. **Time distortion:** A few minutes can feel like an eternity. Anchor for the client: *"Pharmacokinetics have a clear physiological limit. This is temporary, and it will pass."*

---

## 4. Navigating Difficult Material: "Trust, Let Go, Be Open"

Teach the client the core clinical triad (W. Richards, Johns Hopkins):
1. **Trust:** Rely on the prepared setting and the mind's capacity to process experience.
2. **Let Go:** Fighting or analyzing at the peak escalates anxiety; releasing control reduces tension.
3. **Be Open:** Turn toward whatever arises.

> **Anchoring instruction for the client:**  
> *"If a frightening image, wall of fear, or heavy emotion appears — do not run. In an altered state, avoidance intensifies anxiety. Turn toward it with curiosity and ask: 'What are you showing me? What lies behind this?' With steady acceptance, fear usually loses its grip."*

---

## 5. Touch Consent Protocol

In altered states, suggestibility and vulnerability increase markedly, sharpening the power asymmetry between therapist and client. Physical touch is therefore a **high-risk clinical intervention** requiring strict boundaries (McNamee et al., 2023):
- **Right to a *zero-touch* session:** The client may decline all physical contact in advance.
- **Co-therapist or recording:** When supportive touch is planned, the gold standard of safety is a two-facilitator model (or audio/video recording with prior written consent).
- **Permitted grounding zones (with consent only):** palm (hand-holding), static touch on the shoulder, or feet (through blanket/socks). Any touch to the chest, abdomen, pelvis, thighs, face, or spontaneous hugging is strictly prohibited.
- **"Touch test" during preparation:**
  - Place a hand gently on the client's shoulder for a few seconds.
  - Check-in: "How does this feel? Does it create tension? Would you like this option available during the session?"
- **Dual consent & stop signal:** Even with signed consent, always ask verbally in the moment before touching. Saying "Stop" or pulling away ends contact immediately without questioning.
